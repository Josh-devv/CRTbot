## 1. The system in one sentence

A **three-layer top-down model** — **Directional Bias (Monthly/Weekly/Daily) → Context (H4) →
Execution (M15)** — traded only in the **London-open and New-York-open kill zones**, entering on
**order-block formation** at the **order-block open**, with stop at the **order-block extreme**
and a **fixed 3R target**, **maximum one trade per day**, skipping news days and
exception-protocol days.

## 3. Layer 1 — Directional Bias (Ch3)

Two components: **Structure** (the idea) and **Narrative** (the bias).

### 3.1 Structure component

1. **Macro directional context** (Monthly level):
   - **Quarterly Shift framework** + **Seasonal Tendencies**.
   - Both must **agree**, otherwise the hypothesis is **dumped** (no bias).
   - "The best time to be bullish is the start of the month; the best time to be bearish is the
     end of the month." (seasonal tendency, evidenced across 10+ years.)
2. **Validate/invalidate the hypothesis** with **Market Structure + Liquidity Metrics**:
   - Key highs/lows (relevant, untested, external/institutional levels).
   - **Bullish** = price breaks highs and **rejects** lows.
   - **Bearish** = price breaks lows and **rejects** highs.
   - "Price cannot close twice below the lows." Repeated failure to hold below a low = the low is
     protected → bullish.
3. **Refine with Market Profiles** (the correct *type* of move):
   - **Ranging** (consolidation / re-accumulation / re-distribution).
   - **Manipulation** (false move / sweep).
   - **Expansion** (the real move).
   - **Retracement**.
   - PO3 / AMD (Accumulation → Manipulation → Distribution) sequencing.

### 3.2 Narrative component

1. **Define the active (most relevant) dealing range.**
   - Built from the **most recent expansion swing** (needs one significant high + one
     significant low).
   - Sets **premium/discount** position and the **short-term and long-term DOL** (Draw On
     Liquidity).
2. **Identify the appropriate key levels** *within* the dealing range — the specific level price
   will use as its catalyst (order block, breaker, mitigation, etc.).
3. **Connect origin → destination.** That line = the **narrative** = the bias.

Then **refine the whole process down each timeframe** (Monthly → Weekly → Daily).

**Significant high/low (from breakdowns):**
- A **high** is *significant* if it **tested an HTF key level** OR it **led to a low being
  broken**.
- A **low** is *significant* if it **purged liquidity**, **reacted to a key level**, **filled an
  FVG** (e.g. in the opening gap), or showed **SMT**.
- Use the **most recent** clear significant swing; overlay these rules rather than hunting old
  highs/lows.

## 5. Layer 3 — Execution (M15) (Ch6)

Five components: **timeframe selection, entry triggers, entry confirmation, order placement,
trade management.**

### 5.1 Entry triggers (what starts active monitoring)
Any of:
- **Key-level reaction** (price reacts at the identified key level).
- **Liquidity purge** of the CRT / candle range.
- **SMT divergence** (one correlated asset takes the liquidity, the other fails → divergence).

Triggers must occur at a **key time** (§9).

### 5.2 Entry confirmation (what validates the entry)
Primary is **order-block formation** (his go-to). Acceptable alternatives: **FVG formation** or
**MSS (market-structure shift)**. Plus **timing validation** (the key time).

### 5.3 Order placement
- **Entry:** at the **order-block OPEN.**
- **Stop loss:** at the **order-block LOW** (long) / **order-block HIGH** (short).
- **Take profit:** **always a fixed 3R.** "I never change this."

### 5.4 Trade management
- **Never** move the stop loss.
- **Never** move the take profit.
- **No scaling**, no partials, no break-even moves, no trailing.
- Ride the trade to TP or SL.
- **Maximum one trade per day.**
- Trade **only the London open and the New York open kill zones.**

## 6. Order blocks (Ch4)

**Definition** — an order block is a candle (or group of candles) that:
1. Shows **one** of these behaviors:
   - **Purging liquidity**, or
   - **Filling liquidity** (testing a key level), or
   - **Generating liquidity** (a CRT that itself gets purged); and
2. Is **engulfed** by a candle.

- **Body vs wick:** if price closes above only the **body** → the **body** is the OB (isolated
  candle); if price closes above the **wick** → use the **wick** (the whole candle).
- **Single vs multiple candles:** if price closes above several **consecutive** candles as a
  whole → those consecutive candles are **one** order block.

**High-probability OB** (all four):
1. Aligned with **HTF bias**;
2. In **premium of a bearish** dealing range / **discount of a bullish** dealing range;
3. **Forms** at a **key time**;
4. **Gets tested** at a **key time**.

**Propulsion block:** an order block formed by **testing a previous order block**.

A valid OB requires **behavior + engulfment**. Missing either = invalid. A candle that merely
engulfs without one of the three behaviors is **not** an OB.

## 7. CSD, liquidity purge, and SMT (Ch4)

### 7.1 CSD vs liquidity purge
Rejection below a low / above a high, relative to the **relative DOL**:
- Rejection **BEFORE** the relative DOL is reached → **liquidity purge** (price continues; not a
  shift).
- Rejection **AFTER** the relative DOL is reached → **valid CSD** (change of state of delivery,
  potential shift).

### 7.2 CSD vs MS shift
- **CSD** = a structural shift that occurs **after** the relative DOL is reached, at a **key
  time**, often coupled with **SMT**.
- **MSS** = a raw chart pattern; on its own it cannot distinguish a real shift from noise.

### 7.3 SMT (Smart Money Technique / divergence)
High-probability SMT requires **all** of:
1. Aligned with **HTF bias**;
2. Forms at an **HTF key level**;
3. Forms at a **key time**.

**Confirmation:** an SMT is confirmed when followed by a **valid CSD** or a **valid order block**
on the lower timeframe.

**Correlated instruments used:** **EURUSD ↔ GBPUSD**, and **EURUSD ↔ DXY** (DXY as the dollar
reference). Divergence = either EURUSD or GBPUSD takes the liquidity and the other fails to.

## 8. Exception protocols (Ch4, Ch7) — do NOT invent others

1. **4-day protocol:** after **4 consecutive days** moving one direction, the **next day** is
   likely a **counter move**.
2. **Weekly-profile protocol:** if the weekly profile / a news event (**CPI, NFP, FOMC**) has
   already delivered the move, expect a **short-term counter move**. Includes **TGIF** (weekly
   expansion already done → Thursday/Friday counter move).
3. **Asian-range protocol:** if Asia expanded early, London/NY will often move **counter** to
   rebalance.

Each must be paired with other confluences. **Sham's personal action: skip the day entirely**
(stay flat) when an exception is in play.

## 9. Time (Ch4)

- **Time is the LAST validating factor**, not a magic reversal clock. Key times are **windows of
  opportunity**. If bias + context + entry model don't align, time alone = no trade. If the
  other layers align and time agrees → high-confidence trade.
- **Kill zones:** **London open** and **New York open**. Only these.
- **Key macros:** **3:00 AM**, **9:00 AM**.
- **4H candle open/close "timed ranges"** — Sham deliberately keeps specific hours private;
  treat as a configurable list, not hard-coded magic.
- **No trading on NFP / CPI / FOMC days.**

## 10. Anticipatory skills — the expansion-day test (Ch5)

Before trading each day, decide **"is today an expansion day?"** using:
- **Weekly profile**,
- **Weekly & Daily OHLC framework**, and
- **Asian session behavior**.

Heuristics:
- If **Asia consolidated** → **London expansion** likely.
- If **Asia already expanded** → London is low-probability; **New York** is the true expansion.
- **Not every day expands.** Trade the day, not the week; be selective.

## 12. Terminology (working glossary)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CRT** | Candle Range Theory — a candle range whose wick purges liquidity, then the body continues. |
| **DOL** | Draw On Liquidity — the liquidity pool price is drawn to (the destination). |
| **Dealing range** | The active range between the most recent significant high and low. |
| **Premium / discount** | Upper / lower half of the dealing range. |
| **Key level** | A specific level within the dealing range that acts as the catalyst. |
| **Order block** | Candle(s) showing purge/fill/generate-liquidity behaviour **and** engulfed (§6). |
| **Propulsion block** | OB formed by testing a previous OB. |
| **Breaker** | A key level (failed OB) used after a shift. |
| **Mitigation** | Return to a prior imbalance/level. |
| **Liquidity purge / sweep** | Wick through a level that takes stops and rejects. |
| **Filling liquidity** | Price testing a key level. |
| **Generating liquidity** | A CRT that itself becomes the liquidity that is purged. |
| **CSD** | Change of State of Delivery — post-DOL structural shift at a key time (§7). |
| **MSS** | Market Structure Shift (raw pattern; weaker than CSD). |
| **SMT** | Divergence between correlated instruments (§7.3). |
| **PO3 / AMD** | Accumulation → Manipulation → Distribution. |
| **IRL / ERL** | Internal Range Liquidity / External Range Liquidity. |
| **TGIF** | Weekly profile where expansion is complete → late-week counter move. |
| **KOD / OTE** | Mentioned by Sham but not operationally defined in these chapters — out of scope for v1. |
